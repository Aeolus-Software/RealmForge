\textbf{Andreas:}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item When writing this deliverable, our team worked well to synchronize 
	our decisions between sections. While I primarily focused on the FMEA 
	table, I was able to discuss decisions my teammates had made on other vital 
	sections (such as System Boundaries and Components) with them, clarify 
	uncertainties, and use the output of our discussions to guide my work.
	\item One pain point I experienced during this deliverable was finding 
	suitable failure modes and hazards for our software, since it is intended 
	to be a video game (it does not have many obvious physical safety risks). 
	This was resolved by considering other ways failure of the system could 
	result in harm, such as it being inaccessible to users with photosensitive 
	epilepsy, or colourblind users. It was also found that other failures of 
	the system could result in harm (in the expanded sense used in this 
	project), by frustrating users and degrading the user experience overall.
	\item Some risks had been considered before, such as failures of the system 
	resulting in corrupted user save data, or unexpected behavior (that could 
	frustrate the user), but specific risks concerning colourblind users and 
	users with photosensitive epilepsy were newly found and formalized. These 
	risks were found by considering the ways in which failures of the system 
	could cause harm to specific user groups, if they were not accounted for in 
	the design (as these risks could be missed without careful consideration). 
	This led to finding a physical safety hazard (the potential for epileptic 
	seizures in those with photosensitive epilepsy), as well as a potential 
	risk of preventing colourblind users from playing the game as others can.
	\item One risk can involve the risk of preventing certain users from being 
	able to use a software product (as discussed above, in regards to 
	accessibility). This risk is important to consider because it can result in 
	real harm; users with specific conditions can feel alienated when they 
	aren't able to use or enjoy software products that others do. Another risk 
	involves the risk of users becoming confused or frustrated when using the 
	software product (as discussed in the FMEA analysis, when users' saved 
	progress is lost, or the user interface is difficult to understand). This 
	must be considered, as if most users become frustrated when using a 
	software product, it will fail commercially/publicly in favour of other 
	products that are designed with the user in mind.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Humna:}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item As a group, we communicated well, and the team had a good understanding of the overall project. My role for the Hazard Analysis was to complete Section 3, System Boundaries and Components. For this section, I broke RealmForge into its different components and considered how they interact with one another and the external environment. During the previous stages of the project, our group had already discussed the game’s overall functionality and the features we wanted to include. As a result, I used our previous discussions to identify and describe the system components. Our group was also organized throughout the process. We implemented a review system where team members created pull requests that were reviewed before merging. This helped ensure everyone’s work was checked and any necessary changes were addressed before finalizing the deliverable.
	\item The main pain point I experienced was making sure the components aligned with the team’s shared vision for RealmForge. When reviewing my section, I realized that some of the ideas I had included were potential features rather than confirmed components of the game. However, through discussions with the group, I was able to go back and make changes where needed. This reinforced the importance of reviewing our work as a group and having discussions to maintain consistency and have a shared understanding of the project.
	\item Some risks we identified before working on the Hazard Analysis included the game potentially crashing and interrupting game sessions. Another risk we considered was issues with input devices, such as the game not recognizing or responding to a user’s input method. One major risk we identified while working on this document was issues with player progression and how players move through the game. Another important risk we discussed further was progress not being saved correctly. Since progress is an important part of RealmForge, this could significantly affect the player’s experience if they end up losing their progress between gaming sessions and having to start over.
	\item Two important non-physical software risks are loss of user data and issues with the overall game functionality. Loss of data is a major concern because players could lose their saved progress and have to restart parts of the game. Game functionality issues are also important, as crashes or a lack of input detection could disrupt sessions and negatively affect the gameplay experience. Both risks are important and should be considered to ensure RealmForge provides a functional and enjoyable experience for users. 
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Owen:}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item When writing this deliverable, I think the team communicated well 
	when working on different sections. The team asked for help when needed, 
	and noticed when certain portions of divided work relied on other parts. 
	The intro, scope and purpose were all intertwined with the other sections, 
	with discussion taking place to ensure things fit together.

	\item Some pain points that were experienced during this deliverable were 
	time management, and finding adequate content to discuss in a relatively low 
	risk, non safety critical system. Since the project is a video game it was harder 
	to find physical risks, and this was resolved by including hazards/risks that are 
	not immediately obvious, such as colourblind accessibility and interactions with 
	photosensitive epilepsy. The pain point of time management was mainly because 
	of the deliverable going through reading week, where schedules are uncertain 
	and much different from a typical school week. This was resolved by an increase 
	in Microsoft Teams activity, ensuring everyone was in the loop.

	\item Some risks were thought of before this document, including loss of save 
	data, game crashes, and other unintended behavior. These were all immediately 
	identifiable risks that came about when initial discussions of developing a game 
	were happening. Other, more specific risks came about later (during this document), 
	such as users with photosensitive epilepsy having seizures due to flashing lights 
	(this was discussed as a possibility in lecture). Specific risks such as these required 
	extra thought about the design of the game, and not just the implementation.

	\item 2 types of risk (other than physical) for software products include:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Barriers to Entry - Various design choices can cause barriers to entry 
		for some users. For example, if similar colours are used and intended to be 
		differentiated, this can cause colourblind users not to be able to interact with 
		the game as intended.

		\item Unsatisfied Users - Many design choices affect users' enjoyment of the 	
		game. Increased frustration/confusion in figuring out how to interact with the 
		game can cause users to be unsatisfied with their experience, which can lead 
		to consequences like negative game reputation/reviews or game refunds for paid games. 
	\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
